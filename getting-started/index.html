Getting started

Plan: what to learn to start working on agendas

This is our recommendation for what to learn to start working on new or existing theoretical AI safety agendas as fast as possible. We think it is enough to get started. Each step is covered in detail below.

  1. Motivation: the extinction-risk readings below. Takes ~1.5 hours.
  2. AI safety background: skim the BlueDot curricula. Reading them in full can take up to 12 full-time days, but with LLM summaries and skipping lower-priority readings, it takes much less.
  3. Neural networks and transformers: the basics below. Coding is optional. Takes 2–5 full-time days, depending on how deep you want to dive.
  4. Existing theoretical agendas: the Iliad Intensive curriculum (20 days) or the MISO talks (10 days). Both are self-contained and their material likely overlaps a lot, so it isn’t clear which is better to do first; either is a fine start. The MISO recordings aren’t public yet: the organisers have shared them with us, and we are watching them together in WHISKERS (7–18 December). We also plan to run a lecture series similar to the Iliad Intensive at the Faculty of Mathematics in January. For a short overview of existing theoretical agendas, see our Research agendas and Organisations pages.
  5. Next steps: start working on an existing or new agenda on your own, apply to a theoretical AI safety fellowship, or apply directly to the organisations. If you need funding for independent research, see our funding section.

Steps 2 and 3 can be done in any order or in parallel. Steps 1–3 are our short version of the Iliad Intensive prerequisites. Their list is very broad, and you don’t need all of it to start; in particular, we consider coding optional.

1. Motivation

ShowHide ↓
  • Existential Risk from AI: An Exposition for Mathematicians by Xiaoyu He (~1 hour): the case for existential risk, written in the style of a maths paper.
  • How worried should we be about extinction risk from AI? by Timothy Gowers (~30 min): a mathematician’s perspective on extinction risk, with a further-reading list of introductions for mathematicians.
  • Optional: If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies by Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares (book, ~7 hours): argues that the race to superhuman AI risks extinction.

2. AI safety background

ShowHide ↓
  • BlueDot courses: AGI Strategy (5 days) covers general AI safety; Technical AI Safety (6 days) is an intro to empirical technical work, which is still useful if you want to do theoretical AI safety research. You can apply to a course, or just read the curriculum, which is open. There are many readings, so we suggest skimming them with an LLM.
  • CAISH Alignment Fellowship (5 weeks, 3–5 hours a week): a local, in-person reading group in Cambridge. An alternative to the BlueDot courses, not an addition. Covers less material.

3. Neural networks and transformers

ShowHide ↓

Use this list or go through the deep learning section of the Iliad prerequisites.

  • 3Blue1Brown: Neural networks, chapters 1–3 (1 hour): what a neural network is and how it learns. Optional: chapters 4–7 (1.5 hours).
  • Core concepts (loss functions, backpropagation, stochastic gradient descent, overfitting): ask an LLM to explain them.
  • ARENA: inputs and outputs of a transformer: how transformers, the architecture behind LLMs, work.
  • Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF): the original RLHF paper (don’t read it in full; ask an LLM to explain it). Also ask an LLM about reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR).
  • Optional: Deep Dive into LLMs like ChatGPT by Andrej Karpathy (~3.5 hours): how LLMs are built and trained, end to end. Long, but well worth it; Karpathy is one of the best engineers and explainers in the AI field.
  • Optional: coding. You probably don’t need to learn it. Most theoretical agendas need little or no code, and an LLM can write what you need. Learning to code well takes a lot of time, so we don’t recommend spending time on it now. So very optional: PyTorch: Learn the Basics.

4. Existing theoretical agendas

ShowHide ↓
  • Iliad Intensive: a 1-month programme; apply, or you can just read its curriculum. Probably the biggest compilation of theoretical AI safety material right now, across many agendas. Its curriculum includes coding days. If you’re working through it on your own to start on a theoretical agenda, we recommend skipping those and focusing on the maths.
  • MISO: about 35 recorded talks on the mathematics of AI safety, given by researchers from theoretical AI safety labs on their own research, 9–20 November 2026 at MAISI. The MISO organisers have kindly shared the recordings with us, and our WHISKERS programme is about watching them together.

General links

ShowHide ↓
  • LessWrong and the AI Alignment Forum: where most AI safety research is posted and discussed.
  • AI safety map: all AI safety organisations and fellowships in one place.
  • Shallow Review of Technical AI Safety: a 2025 overview of 80+ research agendas, both empirical and theoretical, and who works on them.
  • 80,000 Hours job board: nearly all AI safety jobs are posted here (select AI safety as the area).
  • Don’t Worry About the Vase by Zvi Mowshowitz: AI safety news. If you want to follow only one AI safety news source, make it this one. Zvi’s posts are very long, so we recommend just reading the introduction and skimming the section titles.
