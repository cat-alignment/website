About

Timelines are short. Several lines of evidence point this way: general AI capability scores keep climbing, the length of software tasks AI agents can complete has been doubling about every 4 months, AI is now solving open maths problems, and METR’s recent report found Anthropic’s research is progressing about twice as fast as before, mostly because of AI use in R&D. Based on these trends, forecasters have been shortening their timelines, with many now placing significant probability on artificial general intelligence (AGI) arriving by the end of 2027, unless AI development is deliberately slowed. If AGI does come that soon, there probably isn’t enough time to develop theoretical agendas that align AGI, and policy work matters more.

Why theoretical research then? There’s still a good chance we have more time until AGI, especially if AI development slows down or pauses, possibly enough for theory to pay off. Right now there are only a few theoretical agendas, and only a couple of hundred people work on them. Each agenda might succeed in aligning AGI, but the chances for any single one are slim. So we urgently need more agendas and more people working on them. There is likely still low-hanging fruit, in existing directions and new ones. In a field this small, every additional mathematician can make a real difference.

Why Cambridge? The Centre for Mathematical Sciences has some of the strongest mathematicians anywhere, and that’s who theoretical AI safety needs.

  • If you’re a PhD student, postdoc or faculty member, you already have a position, so you can explore new agendas without competing for the few places in existing organisations.
  • If you’re an undergraduate or master’s student, you can learn the arguments deeply, become a local expert on a topic (the field is young, so this takes months, not years), and help bring top talent from maths, physics and computer science into AI safety.

Are you asking me to change my whole career? No. We’re asking you to spend serious time on theoretical AI safety for the next 6 months. If AI progress doesn’t slow down, in that time you’ll see enough wonders and terrors to judge for yourself whether AI safety work is worth continuing. If it hits a wall (we see no evidence of this, but would be happy if it does), you can easily switch back to your current career track.

We may still have a chance to influence the future. Let’s give it a shot.
