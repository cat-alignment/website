About

The problem

The current rate of progress of AI capabilities far exceeds that of alignment research. We need to build the technical AI safety research field to move with sufficient speed and capacity to match the pace of the capabilities frontier. We see this as necessary to robustly align frontier AI models in safe and well-understood ways.

This likely can’t be solved without pacing the frontier labs, which puts theoretical alignment research against real-world constraints. Theoretical safety research has a low probability of success, like many pursuits in AI safety. However, conditional on success, it is likely to have a very large impact on the alignment of future AI systems.

Currently only around 100 people work on theoretical alignment research. Magnitudes more with the requisite skills are highly concentrated in academic institutions like Cambridge and are underinformed on the risks, though have a visceral sense for the rate of AI progress and an eagerness to learn more. The raw materials are there to build the research institutes and agendas we will need, yet aspiring mathematicians are currently encouraged to consider a surprisingly narrow range of career paths.

What is CAT and how can we help?

The CAT (Cambridge Alignment Theory) Research Group is an AI safety fieldbuilding organisation, focused on engaging mathematicians in theoretical research. We started as a small group of Cambridge maths students trying to build visibility and engagement for AI safety, and targeting the highly concentrated talent in Cambridge towards theoretical alignment research. We hope to build a culture encouraging mathematicians into AI safety, both in the student body and in faculty.

Our longer term aims are to build an organisation acting as a pipeline into theoretical research and facilitating new research agendas. This combines a large multiplier with low hanging fruit: mathematicians are acutely aware of current AI capabilities progress, and are generally eager to learn more.

Who is it for?

Mathematicians and physicists who are curious about AI safety and want to know where to start. Our initial talks will be overviews of the field and the pathways into alignment research, targeted at a general mathematical audience.

The WHISKERS programme is aimed at producing lecturers for a lecture series in Lent and therefore we expect the median participant to be a PhD student, but we encourage anyone interested to attend, from undergrad to faculty!

Where are we now?

Our initial talks are coming up soon, and we are currently accepting applications for the WHISKERS programme. CAT is a very new group, and we are trying to grow fast, so if you are interested and want to join us, do get in touch!
